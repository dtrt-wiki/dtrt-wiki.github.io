Stray Dogs

A group of tattooed enforcers that invaded and took charge of the Outer Backstreets of District 12. Members have named their origin as the Backstreets of District 22, and an account of a night encounter quotes one of them describing the group as the terror of that district. They hold ground where the fog is thinnest, and the local syndicates that were already there answered to them.

Organisation

Testimony describes the group entering District 12 as six packs of roughly fifty members each. A Pack Leader commands a pack. Above the leaders is a Handler, referred to by title only, whose name has not been recorded.

Five of the leaders are named in testimony: Rocco, Marco, Vito, Valentina and Bosco. Valentina is described as the strongest of them and as a known figure in District 22 before the move. A sixth leader appears only through an uncertain recollection, offered as “Mario or Marius” by a speaker who admitted he could not remember. No account places the remaining leaders anywhere in the district.

Relationship with the Death’s Head

The Stray Dogs arrived as an invading force and took charge of the region. The Death’s Head, the local biker syndicate that had held the district’s territory and checkpoints, were overpowered and answered to them afterwards. The arrangement is described as submission rather than alliance.

Because the Death’s Head were the locals, they carried out the work while the Stray Dogs gave the orders. That is recorded as a standing grievance on the Death’s Head side, and it did not survive Bosco’s death.

The Pound

The Pound was a detention facility used to hold troublemakers and anyone the syndicate wanted off the street. Inmates were kept there for holding and, in some cases, for eventual recruitment into the Stray Dogs.

It stayed open on the Stray Dogs’ orders, with the Death’s Head doing the work of running it. Holding and recruiting shared one building, and the facility drew recruits into the group that had taken the region.

The breakout and Bosco’s death

The Pound was broken open by its inmates. A representative of the Kurokumo Clan settled the situation.

Bosco died in that disturbance, in territory controlled by the Death’s Head. Accounts describe him engaging four people, killing one and wounding two, before being shot down, with a swordsman ending the fight afterwards. He is also described as having neglected his duties beforehand, throwing people through windows rather than watching what he was answerable for.

In punishment for permitting the escape, several ranking Death’s Head members were executed by the Kurokumo Clan. Road Captain Jodi, who held the Southwest sector, was among them.

With Bosco dead and the local leadership removed, organization across the region destabilized. The Death’s Head fragmented within that area and the Stray Dogs lost their hold on it.

Bosco’s Pack Remnants

Bosco’s death left his pack without a leader. It broke into smaller groups instead of passing intact to another Pack Leader, and its members have since been recorded scattered across the district.

One of these groups settled into an apartment complex. Lupo rose to lead it, and its members no longer use the Stray Dogs name, stating that Lupo holds the leash. The group appears to be absorbing refugees and smaller syndicates.

Augmentation

Their main weapon is subdermal ink. Each pack has a tuner who applies and maintains it. The tuner is not a member of the pack and is lent to the Pack Leader for the duration of the operation.

A tattoo parlor has been recorded on the seventh floor of a building above Summit Accounting and Blue Rock Holdings, and below a hair salon. The artist there is not a member, works with Stray Dog ink, calls the group bad for business, and was not accepting new work at the last visit.

Prices quoted at the parlor:

  • Full body, double pattern: 3,000,000 Ahn
  • Per limb pair, two pattern: 750,000 Ahn
  • Single limb, single pattern: 250,000 Ahn, 200,000 for regulars

Credit is offered to anyone holding insurance, syndicate backing or a Fixer licence.

Observations

The ink does nothing until it is activated. Members engaged before that point are recorded as markedly easier to put down. Leaders appear to be chosen for fighting ability rather than for command, and their decisions in the field have been recorded as inconsistent. Packs keep to their own boundaries, and no account describes one crossing into another’s ground.
