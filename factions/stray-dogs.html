Stray Dogs

A subsidiary Syndicate of The Thumb originating and dominating the Backstreets of District 22 (V Corp). A pack of violent, punk-aesthetic thugs who control territory through force and augmented bodies. Sent by The Thumb to expand into District 12 following the L Corp collapse.

The Stray Dogs operate begrudgingly in District 12. Territorial by nature and reluctant to expand, they are undertaking this expedition under orders from the Thumb rather than ambition.

Origin

The Stray Dogs originate from District 22, where The Thumb’s influence dominates. V Corp governs District 22 and borders the back side of L Corp’s former territory and District 12, making expansion geographically natural.

They were sent by The Thumb to take up and control territory in District 12 following the L Corp collapse — exploiting the power vacuum left behind. As a subsidiary, they answer to the Thumb in the same way the Kurokumo Clan does. These subsidiaries work alongside each other when ordered, but they are not allies. A common superior does not make them friends. Conflict between subsidiaries is tolerated so long as it does not interfere with the overall objective.

Expedition to District 12

The Thumb ordered an expeditionary force of approximately 300 members into District 12. Not so large as to leave District 22 vulnerable, but substantial enough to demonstrate commitment. The force was drawn not from the Stray Dogs’ finest, but from members who were useful enough to keep alive but problematic enough to remove from home territory. The expedition serves a dual purpose: securing a foothold in the fallen district, and shedding dead weight.

Reinforcements from District 22, if requested, would take one to two weeks to arrive. Communication with the home territory is infrequent, as the Stray Dogs would prefer to have nothing to do with the actual territory they are being forced to take.

Territory

The Stray Dogs operate in the Backstreets of District 12 — the campaign’s primary location. Their expeditionary force is concentrated in the Outer Backstreets, where the fog is least concentrated and resembles an overcast gray sky. They are invaders and expansionists, pressuring existing District 12 factions — most notably the Death’s Head, whose local leadership they killed and whose territory they encroached upon.

They split their force across the Outer Backstreets territory, working alongside other Thumb subsidiaries (such as the Kurokumo Clan). Each subsidiary operates independently, acting in a land-grab dynamic, asserting dominance over local syndicates and conscripting them into service. The subsidiaries do not particularly care if their “underling” syndicates fight each other, so long as it does not interfere with the overall objective.

Hierarchy

Pack Handler

The expedition is overseen by a single Pack Handler — the one who holds the leashes of the Pack Leaders. The Handler is an older, known member of the Stray Dogs who has fallen out of favor. Their ideals no longer align with the Stray Dogs of the present day, and they were strong-armed into this position. Competent and unpredictable, the Handler was sent precisely because they were capable enough to control the Pack Leaders but expendable enough to be risked on the expedition.

The Handler moves cautiously. The death of a Pack Leader is not something to react to hastily — it could be a mistake, or it could be an organized attack. By being slow to respond, the Handler avoids falling into a potential trap, but this caution comes at the cost of leaving isolated members without direction for extended periods.

Pack Leaders

Below the Handler are six Pack Leaders, each commanding a Pack of roughly 50 members. Pack Leaders earn their position through strength and combat ability rather than administrative skill. This leads to suboptimal decisions and management errors, as a capable fighter is not necessarily a capable commander.

Five of the six Pack Leaders are variants of the same problem: violent, difficult to manage, and sent to District 12 because the Stray Dogs wanted them gone. At least one, however, is mentally capable and administratively adept — a genuine asset who happens to be leading an expedition of undesirables.

Pack Leaders do not cross each other’s territory — territorial boundaries are strictly observed. They are more heavily augmented and experienced than common packmates, with better equipment and more intricate tattoos signifying rank.

Packmates

Common members. Rank-and-file thugs who follow their Pack Leader. They are the expedition’s ground force, augmented through tattoo ink and armed with improvised weapons. Most carry Standard-grade ink, maintained by the Pack’s tuner.

Bosco’s Orphaned Pack

With Bosco’s death and no formal chain of command to reclaim his people, approximately 50 members were left orphaned. Bosco had already split them into smaller, unofficial groups to manage his territory — he was a lone wolf who kept his pack at arm’s length.

Their fates have diverged:

  • 20 feral — Roaming bands with no leadership. These are the most dangerous and unpredictable. They take what they need and move on, indistinguishable from the lawless threat the Outer Backstreets fears most.
  • 15 integrated locally — Pragmatists who have disappeared into District 12, taking work with local syndicates or communities. Hardest to track because they actively do not want to be found.
  • 10 reintegrated with other Packs — The most disciplined of Bosco’s people, who have submitted to new Pack Leaders. Requires initiative and the willingness to accept a new chain of command — not abundant qualities in this force.
  • 5 dead — Killed in the chaos, executed by locals settling old scores, or lost to the fog. Their names will likely never be confirmed.

The Handler has not moved to reclaim them, watching instead to determine whether Bosco’s death was a mistake or an organized attack. In his wariness, the orphaned packmates remain cut off from the syndicate that sent them here.

Body Augmentation: Tattoos

The Stray Dogs’ primary weapon is their own bodies, enhanced through subdermal nanite ink. The technology was originally a Wing Singularity — a proprietary augmentation system owned by a single Wing. When that Wing collapsed and its patent expired, the tech flooded the open market. Every syndicate, workshop, and back-alley artist has been modifying and reproducing it ever since.

Essenai

The generic term for subdermal nanite ink is SNI (Subdermal Nanite Ink), but decades of verbal abbreviation have corrupted it into a word that most people treat as a brand name: Essenai. Many argue about whether Essenai is the best brand or just the most popular, unaware that it was never a brand at all. It is the industry standard, the “acetaminophen” to everyone else’s “Tylenol.”

In practice, “Essenai” refers to the baseline standard-grade ink that most syndicate operations use. Every expedition, including this one, carries Essenai as its primary stock.

Quality and Modification

Tattoo quality is not a strict tier system. It is a function of the brand (the raw ink), the modification (the tuning), and the artist (the application).

  • Subpar — Cheaply mass-produced ink where drawbacks have not been engineered out. Diluted or poorly replicated ink sold for cheap. Tradeoffs are severe. Some subpar inks are modified to imitate higher grades, running harder but prone to burning out faster.
  • Standard — Does what it says on the tin. Side effects are known and documented, though not always communicated to the customer. What a knowledgeable person buys after doing research. Standard generic Essenai falls here.
  • Prototype — Mostly untested, specialized or experimental ink. Expensive. Trying to eliminate the natural drawbacks of a design, but the price must be paid somewhere. Typically developed by workshops or talented independent tuners.

The brand is the hardware. The ink source, the manufacturer, the raw material. Quality is the modification done to it. An artist can take standard Essenai and make it perform like a prototype through skilled tuning. A cheap artist can ruin premium workshop ink through poor application.

Tattoo Categories

Tattoo ink falls into five broad categories based on how it activates and sustains its effects. Each category represents a philosophy of combat as much as a technical mechanism.

Passive — Always on. The tradeoff is built into the body. The user acknowledges the cost and lives with it, permanently. A packmate with passive reinforcement moves slower or tires faster, but is always armored. Cannot be turned off, so the user learns to carry the weight.

Active — Burst — The user primes the ink and the effect happens in a single short activation. Requires intent, focus, and stamina. The fastest way to develop instinctual use of tattoos, eventually making them feel like a natural extension of the body. Also the fastest way to burn out.

Active — Toggle — The user activates the ink, and it sustains its effect until manually deactivated or the user’s stamina runs out. Avoids the permanent tradeoff of passive tattoos but carries the risk of continuous stamina drain. The user must end the fight before they hit their limit.

Reactive — The ink responds to an external stimulus without conscious input. An impact causes it to harden. A threat causes it to flare. The advantage is speed. The disadvantage is it can be baited or overwhelmed. A clever opponent can trigger a reactive tattoo deliberately, leaving the user exposed during cooldown.

Proactive — The user anticipates a condition and primes the ink. The effect is stored until that condition is met. Requires foresight, analysis, and a clear head under pressure. Flexible but demanding. A veteran who knows the district can use proactive tattoos with devastating precision. A panicking user carries dead ink into a fight.

Tuners and Ink Artists

The Stray Dogs do not rely on external tattoo parlors alone. Each Pack has access to a dedicated tuner — an ink specialist who handles maintenance, modification, and new application. Tuners are not subordinates of the Pack Leader. They are assets loaned by the Pack Handler and managed by the Pack Leader for the duration of the expedition.

This arrangement serves a dual purpose. The Pack gets consistent ink support. The Handler gets eyes inside each Pack. A Pack Leader who mistreats their tuner is a Pack Leader whose behavior the Handler hears about through a channel they cannot control.

Tuners fall into two categories:

  • Exclusive tuners — Bound to the Stray Dogs by contract or loyalty. They develop the cultural style that makes a Stray Dog recognizable on sight. The specific patterns, the punk aesthetic, the way the ink catches the eye before the fight starts. These are the artists who give the syndicate its visual identity.
  • Contract tuners — Independent professionals working a job. No loyalty to the syndicate. They tune standard-issue ink, handle maintenance, process bulk orders. They could walk away at any time, and the Stray Dogs know it, which means the relationship must be managed rather than commanded.

Each Pack Leader uses their tuner differently based on their personality and priorities:

  • Rocco prioritizes pack-wide maintenance. His tuner works on his people before him. If commercial work happens, it is because Rocco asked them to support a local community.
  • Marco pushes his tuner for new applications and modifications. He treats them as a research partner, always looking for the next edge.
  • Vito demands his tuner prioritize him personally. His pack gets the bare minimum. Commercial work goes through Vito, who takes a cut.
  • Valentina burns through tuners. Her burst style is hard on ink and hard on the person applying it. Her current tuner is either new, desperate, or as intense as she is.
  • Marcus has the quietest relationship. Maintenance on a schedule. New applications only when tactically justified. His tuner has the most free time of any in the expedition.

Tattoo Parlor

The Stray Dogs operate a tattoo parlor on the seventh floor of a multi-story building (above Summit Accounting and Blue Rock Holdings, below a hair salon). The independent artist there works with Stray Dog ink but is not a member himself. He offers body augmentation services:

  • Full body double-pattern enhancement: 3,000,000 Ahn
  • Per limb pair (two-pattern): 750,000 Ahn
  • Single limb single-pattern: 250,000 Ahn (200,000 for regulars)
  • Credit available for those with insurance, Syndicate backing, or Fixer license
  • The artist noted the Stray Dogs are “bad for business” due to the trouble they attract

Equipment

  • Street clothing with punk aesthetic — ragged, torn, deliberately rough
  • Improvised weapons — pipes, bats, machetes, brass knuckles, and similar
  • Body augmentation tattoos — their primary combat enhancement
  • Accessories — items that augment or mimic tattoo powers

Faction Conflicts

  • Death’s Head — The Stray Dogs killed the local Death’s Head leadership (shotgun woman and her second-in-command) and have been expanding into their territory. Death’s Head members are fleeing.
  • The Community — Some residents are scared of heading out due to Stray Dog activity in the area.
  • Lavender/Agent Scoot — Has a grudge against the Stray Dogs and sought to recover relics from groups holding them.
  • Local Syndicates — The Stray Dogs conscript local operations into service. These underlings are permitted to fight among themselves so long as the overall objective is not compromised.
  • Kurokumo Clan — Fellow Thumb subsidiary. Not allies. A common superior, but the groups are not friends.
  • Night Witches — The Stray Dogs have encroached on Night Witches territory, with feral remnants of Bosco’s pack causing disappearances that the Night Witches are being blamed for.

Strengths

  • Territorial organization with clear pack structure
  • Body augmentation tattoos provide enhanced combat abilities
  • Accessories that mimic tattoo powers add versatility
  • Fierce loyalty and pack bonds
  • Willingness to use violence to expand territory
  • Dedicated tuners for ink maintenance and modification
  • Expeditionary force of ~300 with additional local conscripts

Weaknesses

  • Tattoos only work when activated — catching them off guard neutralizes their advantage
  • Pack Leaders are combat-promoted, not administratively capable — suboptimal decisions are common
  • Pack Handler moves cautiously, leading to slow response times and isolated members
  • The force was drawn from undesirables — loyalty and competence are inconsistent
  • Reinforcements from District 22 take 1-2 weeks to arrive
  • Subsidiary status means the Stray Dogs can be undermined by other Thumb subsidiaries without consequence
  • The Handler’s loyalty to the current Stray Dogs is questionable — they are in this position because their ideals no longer align with the organization
  • Dependence on tuners means a disrupted ink supply chain cripples combat capability
